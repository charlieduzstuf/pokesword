Correct the census: 5,212 "mismatches" were link-time placeholders, not bodies

## What was wrong

The census compared a compiled tail-call thunk against the original **byte for
byte**. A thunk never matches that way:

    original bytes  0c000014   b #0x210
    candidate bytes 00000014   b #0

`b #0` is not a wrong answer. It is the placeholder the compiler emits for a call
the linker has not resolved; the real destination lives in the object's
relocations. `auto_match` has always read it from there and compared *destinations*:

    MH.compare(orig, end, mine, orig_branches=[mangled(want)],
               mine_branches=[relocs[mangled][0]])

So the census was measuring its own artefact and reporting it as a population of
unmatched bodies. Measured on the same 700 candidates:

    before   523 match   176 mismatch   (71.5% byte-match)
    after    648 match    51 mismatch   (92.6% byte-match)

Exactly the 125 thunks changed verdict and nothing else did. `strlit-ret`,
`setter-chain`, `straight`, `indexed-getter` and `copy-chain` are untouched.

Corroboration that the loop was right all along: re-running `auto_match` on
main's tailcall population gives `MATCH 400 / 400 = 100.00%`, and all 5,083
thunks in main's registry already carry byte-identical records with no `verdict`
field. The harvest loop had claimed them; the census was re-deriving them as
"new".

## The real semantic population

    7,158 queued  ->  ~1,946 genuine mismatches

And of the 131 semantic records actually registered, **zero are thunks** -- every
one is an emulated `unicorn differential` verdict, mostly `straight` (115). Those
stand. The static thunk checker was built for a population that does not exist,
which is a tool aimed at a measurement artefact.

The phantom records and the journal that produced them are removed: one sdk thunk
had been merged into the registry, and the 8,268-line journal (7,728 of them
thunks) is deleted rather than committed.

## The lesson, which is the fourth time this session

A number that *rises* when a tool is added invites the reading that the tool found
something. This one fell by 5,212 once the tool measured the right thing, and in
the meantime it had produced 131 registered records, a committed 8,268-line
journal, and a plausible-looking "+18.9% semantic". Every one of those was an
artefact of comparing a placeholder against a real address.

The tell was available the whole time and was not read: the candidate bytes were
*always* `b #0`, on all 5,212 of them. A population with a single repeated byte
pattern is not a population of distinct problems; it is one bug sampled 5,212
times. Uniqueness is evidence, and there was a uniqueness check that was not run.

The second tell: `--report` writes only `verdict == "match"` rows, so a
verification run claiming 400/400 and a census claiming 5,212 failures were
describing the same bodies. Two tools, opposite verdicts, no reconciliation. The
reconciliation was one command away the whole time.

## Journal resilience kept

The journal work was sound and is kept: it saved 2,256 verdicts across a restart
that would otherwise have lost them, and `--merge-only` makes recovery invocable
on its own. `--queue` had been `required=True`, which meant the recovery path
could not be exercised without a queue -- a recovery path tested only when
everything else has already gone wrong.
